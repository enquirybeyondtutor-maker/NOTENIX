gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p1-01 · Atoms and bonding · Atomic structure · difficulty 1
An atom has 11 protons and 12 neutrons. What is its mass number?
  1. 11
  2. 12
  3. 23
  4. 1
Answer C. The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus: 11 + 12 = 23. The number of protons alone, 11, is the atomic number.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p1-02 · Atoms and bonding · Periodic table · difficulty 2
Why does the reactivity of the Group 1 metals increase going down the group?
  1. The atoms have more outer electrons
  2. The atoms get smaller, so the nucleus attracts electrons more strongly
  3. The metals become harder to melt
  4. The outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is lost more easily
Answer D. Going down Group 1 the atoms have more electron shells. The single outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, so it is attracted less strongly and lost more easily.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p1-03 · Atoms and bonding · Bonding and structure · difficulty 2
Why does sodium chloride have a high melting point?
  1. It is made of small molecules with weak forces between them
  2. There are strong electrostatic forces between oppositely charged ions in a giant lattice
  3. It contains delocalised electrons that are free to move
  4. Each atom is joined to four others by covalent bonds
Answer B. Sodium chloride is a giant ionic lattice. A lot of energy is needed to overcome the strong electrostatic attraction between the positive sodium ions and negative chloride ions.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p1-04 · Quantities and reactions · Quantitative chemistry · difficulty 2
What is the relative formula mass of calcium carbonate, $\text{CaCO}_3$? (Relative atomic masses: Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16)
  1. 100
  2. 68
  3. 84
  4. 116
Answer A. Add the masses of every atom in the formula: $40 + 12 + (3 \times 16) = 100$.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p1-05 · Quantities and reactions · Quantitative chemistry · difficulty 3
What is the mass of $0.25$ moles of carbon dioxide, $\text{CO}_2$? (Relative atomic masses: C = 12, O = 16)
  1. 176 g
  2. 44 g
  3. 11 g
  4. 7 g
Answer C. The relative formula mass of $\text{CO}_2$ is $12 + (2 \times 16) = 44$. Mass = moles $\times$ formula mass $= 0.25 \times 44 = 11$ g.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p1-06 · Quantities and reactions · Chemical changes · difficulty 2
What are the products when an acid reacts with a metal carbonate?
  1. A salt and hydrogen
  2. A salt, water and carbon dioxide
  3. A salt and water only
  4. A metal oxide and carbon dioxide
Answer B. Acid + metal carbonate gives a salt, water and carbon dioxide. A salt and hydrogen are made when an acid reacts with a metal, and a salt and water when it reacts with an alkali.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p1-07 · Quantities and reactions · Electrolysis · difficulty 3
Molten lead bromide is electrolysed. What forms at the negative electrode (cathode)?
  1. Bromine
  2. Hydrogen
  3. Oxygen
  4. Lead
Answer D. Positive lead ions are attracted to the negative electrode, where they gain electrons and form lead metal. Bromine forms at the positive electrode. There is no water present, so no hydrogen or oxygen forms.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p1-08 · Quantities and reactions · Energy changes · difficulty 2
Which statement describes an exothermic reaction?
  1. Energy is transferred to the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings rises
  2. Energy is taken in from the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings falls
  3. No energy is transferred during the reaction
  4. The products always have more energy than the reactants
Answer A. An exothermic reaction transfers energy to the surroundings, so the surroundings get warmer. The products end up with less energy than the reactants.

gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p2-01 · Rates and organic · Rates and equilibrium · difficulty 2
Why does increasing the temperature increase the rate of a reaction?
  1. The particles get bigger
  2. The activation energy increases
  3. The particles move faster, so they collide more often and with more energy
  4. There are more particles in the same volume
Answer C. At a higher temperature the particles have more energy and move faster. They collide more often, and more of the collisions have at least the activation energy, so more are successful.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p2-02 · Rates and organic · Rates and equilibrium · difficulty 3
In the reaction $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ the forward reaction is exothermic. What happens to the yield of ammonia if the temperature is increased?
  1. It decreases
  2. It increases
  3. It stays the same
  4. It doubles
Answer A. Raising the temperature moves the equilibrium in the endothermic direction to oppose the change. Here that is the reverse reaction, so less ammonia is present at equilibrium.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p2-03 · Rates and organic · Organic chemistry · difficulty 1
What is the general formula of the alkanes?
  1. $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$
  2. $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$
  3. $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+1}\text{OH}$
  4. $\text{C}_n\text{H}_n$
Answer B. Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with the general formula $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$, for example methane $\text{CH}_4$ and ethane $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6$. $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$ is the formula for alkenes.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p2-04 · Rates and organic · Organic chemistry · difficulty 2
Bromine water is shaken with an alkene. What colour change is seen?
  1. Colourless to orange
  2. Orange to blue
  3. Blue to colourless
  4. Orange to colourless
Answer D. Alkenes have a carbon to carbon double bond that reacts with bromine, so orange bromine water turns colourless. With an alkane it would stay orange.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p2-05 · Analysis and the Earth · Chemical analysis · difficulty 2
In a chromatography experiment a spot of dye moves $3.6\ \text{cm}$ and the solvent front moves $8.0\ \text{cm}$. What is the $R_f$ value of the dye?
  1. 2.2
  2. 0.45
  3. 4.4
  4. 0.36
Answer B. $R_f$ is the distance moved by the substance divided by the distance moved by the solvent: $3.6 \div 8.0 = 0.45$. An $R_f$ value is always less than $1$.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p2-06 · Analysis and the Earth · Chemical analysis · difficulty 2
A compound gives a lilac flame in a flame test. Which metal ion does it contain?
  1. Sodium
  2. Calcium
  3. Potassium
  4. Copper
Answer C. Potassium ions give a lilac flame. Sodium gives yellow, calcium gives orange-red and copper gives green.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p2-07 · Analysis and the Earth · Atmosphere · difficulty 2
Which gas makes up about four-fifths of the Earth's atmosphere today?
  1. Nitrogen
  2. Oxygen
  3. Carbon dioxide
  4. Argon
Answer A. The atmosphere is about 78% nitrogen, roughly four-fifths. Oxygen makes up about 21%, with small amounts of argon, carbon dioxide and other gases.
gcse-chemistry-diagnostic-p2-08 · Analysis and the Earth · Using resources · difficulty 2
What does it mean when water is described as potable?
  1. It contains no dissolved substances at all
  2. It has been distilled
  3. It comes from the sea
  4. It is safe to drink
Answer D. Potable water is water that is safe to drink. It is not chemically pure: it still contains low levels of dissolved salts, but harmful microbes have been removed or killed.

Where are the metals found in the periodic table?
  1. On the left and towards the bottom
  2. On the far right only

  4. Only along the top row
Answer A. Metals are on the left and towards the bottom of the periodic table. Non-metals are on the right and towards the top.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p1-04 · Atoms and bonding · Periodic table · difficulty 2
What happens to the reactivity of the halogens going down Group 7?
  1. It increases
  2. It stays the same
  3. It decreases
  4. It increases and then decreases
Answer C. The halogens become less reactive going down the group. Fluorine is the most reactive halogen. This is the opposite of the trend in Group 1.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p1-05 · Atoms and bonding · Bonding and structure · difficulty 1
Ionic bonding happens between which types of element?
  1. Two non-metals
  2. A metal and a non-metal
  3. Two metals
  4. Two noble gases
Answer B. In ionic bonding, metal atoms lose electrons to form positive ions and non-metal atoms gain electrons to form negative ions. The oppositely charged ions attract strongly.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p1-06 · Atoms and bonding · Bonding and structure · difficulty 2
Magnesium forms $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions and chlorine forms $\text{Cl}^-$ ions. What is the formula of magnesium chloride?
  1. $\text{MgCl}_2$
  2. $\text{MgCl}$
  3. $\text{Mg}_2\text{Cl}$
  4. $\text{Mg}_2\text{Cl}_3$
Answer A. The charges must balance. One $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ion needs two $\text{Cl}^-$ ions, so the formula is $\text{MgCl}_2$.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p1-07 · Quantities and reactions · Quantitative chemistry · difficulty 2
What is the relative formula mass of magnesium hydroxide, $\text{Mg(OH)}_2$? (Relative atomic masses: Mg = 24, O = 16, H = 1)
  1. 41
  2. 42
  3. 57
  4. 58
Answer D. The bracket means two OH groups. $M_r = 24 + 2 \times (16 + 1) = 24 + 34 = 58$.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p1-08 · Quantities and reactions · Quantitative chemistry · difficulty 2
Magnesium ribbon is heated in a crucible with a lid lifted now and then. The mass of the solid increases. Why?
  1. Magnesium gets heavier when it is hot
  2. Mass is created in the reaction
  3. Oxygen atoms from the air join with the magnesium
  4. Water evaporates from the crucible
Answer C. Magnesium reacts with oxygen from the air to form magnesium oxide. The mass of the oxygen is added to the solid, so the mass goes up. Mass is still conserved overall.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p1-09 · Quantities and reactions · Chemical changes · difficulty 2
What is formed when an acid reacts with an alkali?
  1. A salt and water
  2. A salt and hydrogen
  3. Carbon dioxide and water
  4. Only water
Answer A. This is neutralisation: acid + alkali → salt + water. Hydrogen ions from the acid react with hydroxide ions from the alkali to make water.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p1-10 · Quantities and reactions · Chemical changes · difficulty 2
How is iron extracted from iron oxide?
  1. By electrolysis of the oxide
  2. By heating the oxide with carbon
  3. By dissolving the oxide in water
  4. By filtering the ore
Answer B. Iron is less reactive than carbon, so carbon can remove the oxygen from iron oxide. The iron oxide is reduced and the carbon is oxidised.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p1-11 · Quantities and reactions · Electrolysis · difficulty 2
During electrolysis, to which electrode do positive ions move?
  1. The positive electrode (anode)
  2. Both electrodes equally
  3. The negative electrode (cathode)
  4. Neither electrode
Answer C. Opposite charges attract, so positive ions move to the negative electrode and negative ions move to the positive electrode.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p1-12 · Quantities and reactions · Energy changes · difficulty 2
During a reaction the temperature of the surrounding solution falls. What type of reaction is this?
  1. Exothermic
  2. Neutralisation
  3. Combustion
  4. Endothermic
Answer D. An endothermic reaction takes in energy from its surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings falls. Sports injury cold packs use endothermic reactions.

Which change would decrease the rate of a reaction?

Answer C. At a lower temperature particles move more slowly, so they collide less often and with less energy. All the other changes increase the rate.

Blue hydrated copper sulfate is heated and turns into white anhydrous copper sulfate. What happens when water is added to the white solid?
  1. Nothing happens
  2. It turns black
  3. It turns blue again and takes in energy
  4. It turns blue again and gives out energy
Answer D. The reaction is reversible. If one direction is endothermic, the reverse is exothermic by the same amount. Heating took energy in, so adding water turns it blue again and releases energy.

What is the formula of methane, the simplest alkane?

What are the products of the complete combustion of a hydrocarbon?

  4. Oxygen and water
Answer A. When a hydrocarbon burns in plenty of oxygen, the carbon is oxidised to carbon dioxide and the hydrogen to water. Carbon monoxide and soot form when there is too little oxygen.
gcse-chemistry-mock-1-p2-06 · Rates and organic · Organic chemistry · difficulty 2
Alkenes are described as unsaturated. What does this mean?

Why do different isotopes of an element have the same chemical properties?

A particle has 12 protons, 12 neutrons and 10 electrons. Which particle is it? (Magnesium has atomic number 12.)

  2. A $\text{Na}^+$ ion
  3. A $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ion
  4. A $\text{Mg}^{2-}$ ion
Answer C. 12 protons means it is magnesium. It has two more protons than electrons, so its charge is $2+$. It is a $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ion.
gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p1-03 · Atoms and bonding · Periodic table · difficulty 3
Why does the reactivity of the halogens decrease going down Group 7?
  1. The atoms lose electrons more easily
  2. The outer shell is further from the nucleus, so an electron is gained less easily
  3. The atoms have more electrons in their outer shell
  4. The molecules become lighter
Answer B. Halogens react by gaining one electron. Further down the group there are more shells, so the outer shell is further from the nucleus and more shielded. The attraction for an extra electron is weaker.
gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p1-04 · Atoms and bonding · Periodic table · difficulty 2
Which property is typical of transition metals but not of Group 1 metals?
  1. Low density
  2. Low melting point
  3. Vigorous reaction with cold water
  4. Forming coloured compounds and acting as catalysts
Answer D. Transition metals such as iron and copper are dense, have high melting points, form ions with different charges and coloured compounds, and are useful catalysts. Group 1 metals are soft, low-density and very reactive.
gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p1-05 · Atoms and bonding · Bonding and structure · difficulty 3
Why does sodium chloride conduct electricity when molten but not when solid?
  1. Electrons are only free when it is molten

  3. When molten its ions are free to move and carry charge
  4. Solid sodium chloride has no ions
Answer C. In the solid the ions are held in fixed positions in the lattice. When it melts or dissolves, the ions can move and carry charge through the liquid.
gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p1-06 · Atoms and bonding · Bonding and structure · difficulty 3
Why are nanoparticles often very effective catalysts?

  3. They contain no atoms

Answer A. Nanoparticles are 1 to 100 nm across, so a large share of their atoms are at the surface. This very high surface area to volume ratio means smaller amounts are needed to catalyse a reaction.
gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p1-07 · Quantities and reactions · Quantitative chemistry · difficulty 3

gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p1-09 · Quantities and reactions · Chemical changes · difficulty 3

gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p1-10 · Quantities and reactions · Chemical changes · difficulty 3

  4. It increases by a factor of 10

gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p1-11 · Quantities and reactions · Electrolysis · difficulty 3
In the extraction of aluminium, the aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite. Why?

  2. Cryolite removes oxygen from the cell
  3. It lowers the operating temperature, saving energy and cost
  4. It makes the aluminium more reactive

gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p1-12 · Quantities and reactions · Energy changes · difficulty 3
In an exothermic reaction, how does the energy released when new bonds form compare with the energy needed to break the bonds in the reactants?
  1. It is less
  2. It is greater
  3. It is exactly the same
  4. No energy is involved in making bonds
Answer B. Breaking bonds takes in energy and making bonds releases energy. In an exothermic reaction more energy is released by making bonds than is taken in to break them, so there is an overall release of energy.

gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p2-01 · Rates and organic · Rates and equilibrium · difficulty 3
A reversible reaction is at equilibrium in a closed system. More of one of the reactants is added. What happens?
  1. The position of equilibrium shifts to the left
  2. The reaction stops
  3. Nothing changes
  4. The position of equilibrium shifts to the right, making more product
Answer D. By Le Chatelier's principle, the system responds to oppose the change. Adding reactant shifts the equilibrium to the right, using up some of the added reactant and making more product.

How does a catalyst increase the rate of a reaction?

  2. It provides a different reaction pathway with a lower activation energy
  3. It gives the particles more energy
  4. It increases the concentration of the reactants
Answer B. A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy. More of the collisions have enough energy to react, so the rate increases.

gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p2-06 · Rates and organic · Organic chemistry · difficulty 2
Compared with long-chain hydrocarbons, short-chain hydrocarbons are...
  1. more flammable, less viscous and have lower boiling points

  4. less flammable and have higher boiling points

gcse-chemistry-mock-2-p2-07 · Analysis and the Earth · Chemical analysis · difficulty 3

How did the amount of carbon dioxide in the Earth's early atmosphere decrease?

  1. The mass of carbon in the product

Which conditions are used in the Haber process to make ammonia?
  1. 25 °C, 1 atmosphere and no catalyst
  2. 450 °C, 200 atmospheres and a platinum catalyst
  3. 1000 °C, 1 atmosphere and an iron catalyst
  4. About 450 °C, 200 atmospheres and an iron catalyst

How does galvanising protect iron from rusting?
  1. It coats the iron in tin
  2. It coats the iron in zinc, which also corrodes in preference to the iron
  3. It paints the iron with copper
  4. It keeps the iron under water
Answer B. Galvanised iron is coated with zinc. The coating keeps out air and water, and because zinc is more reactive than iron, it corrodes instead of the iron even if scratched. This is sacrificial protection.
