Q1 · Cells
Which part of a cell controls its activities and contains genetic material?
  1. Cell membrane
  2. Cytoplasm

Which of these is found in plant cells but NOT in animal cells?
  1. Nucleus
  2. Chloroplasts
  3. Cell membrane
  4. Mitochondria
Why: Chloroplasts (where photosynthesis happens) are only in plant cells. Plant cells also have a cell wall and a permanent vacuole.
Q3 · Cells
What is the job of the cell membrane?
  1. Makes food by photosynthesis
  2. Controls what enters and leaves the cell
  3. Releases energy from glucose
  4. Keeps the cell rigid
Why: The membrane controls movement of substances in and out. The cell wall (in plants) keeps the cell rigid.
Q4 · Cells
What is the correct order of organisation, from smallest to largest?
  1. Tissue → cell → organ → organ system
  2. Cell → tissue → organ → organ system
  3. Cell → organ → tissue → organ system
  4. Organ → tissue → cell → organ system
Why: Similar cells form a tissue, tissues form an organ, and organs work together as an organ system.
Q5 · Particles & states of matter
In which state are particles closest together and only vibrating on the spot?
  1. Solid
  2. Liquid
  3. Gas
  4. All three equally
Why: In a solid the particles are tightly packed in a fixed arrangement and can only vibrate.
Q6 · Particles & states of matter
What is the name for a liquid changing into a gas at its boiling point?
  1. Melting
  2. Condensing
  3. Boiling
  4. Freezing
Why: Boiling is liquid → gas at the boiling point. Condensing is the reverse: gas → liquid.
Q7 · Particles & states of matter
Why can a gas be squashed (compressed) but a solid cannot?
  1. There are big spaces between gas particles
  2. Gas particles are smaller
  3. Gas particles do not move
  4. Solids are always heavier
Why: Gas particles are far apart, so pushing them closer together is possible. Solid particles are already touching.
Q8 · Mixtures & separation
Which method would you use to get salt back from salty water?
  1. Filtration
  2. Magnetism
  3. Evaporation
  4. Sieving
Why: Heating the solution evaporates the water and leaves the salt crystals behind. Filtration cannot remove a dissolved solid.
Q9 · Mixtures & separation
Sand is mixed with water. Which method separates them best?
  1. Evaporation
  2. Filtration
  3. Chromatography
  4. Distillation
Why: Sand does not dissolve, so it is trapped by filter paper while the water passes through.
Q10 · Acids & alkalis
A solution has a pH of 2. What is it?
  1. A strong acid
  2. A weak acid
  3. Neutral
  4. An alkali
Why: pH below 7 is acidic; the lower the number, the stronger the acid. pH 7 is neutral; above 7 is alkaline.
Q11 · Acids & alkalis
What is produced when an acid reacts with an alkali?
  1. A metal and oxygen
  2. Only hydrogen
  3. Only carbon dioxide
  4. A salt and water
Why: This is neutralisation: acid + alkali → salt + water.
Q12 · Forces
What is the unit of force?
  1. Kilogram (kg)
  2. Joule (J)
  3. Newton (N)
  4. Metre per second (m/s)
Why: Forces are measured in newtons, using a newtonmeter (force meter).
Q13 · Forces
A box has a 10 N force pushing it right and a 4 N friction force to the left. What is the resultant force?
  1. 14 N to the right
  2. 6 N to the left
  3. 6 N to the right
  4. 4 N to the left
Why: Opposite forces subtract: $10 - 4 = 6$ N, in the direction of the bigger force (right).
Q14 · Forces
Why does an astronaut weigh less on the Moon than on Earth, even though their mass is the same?
  1. The Moon has no air
  2. The Moon is colder
  3. Mass changes in space
  4. The Moon's gravity is weaker
Why: Weight = mass × gravitational field strength. Mass stays the same, but the Moon's gravity is about one sixth of Earth's.
Q15 · Energy
A battery-powered torch is switched on. Which energy store is being emptied?
  1. Chemical store
  2. Kinetic store
  3. Gravitational store
  4. Elastic store
Why: The battery's chemical store is emptied; energy is transferred electrically and by light (and some by heating).
Q16 · Energy
Which of these is a renewable energy resource?
  1. Coal
  2. Wind
  3. Natural gas
  4. Oil
Why: Wind will not run out. Coal, oil and gas are fossil fuels and are non-renewable.
Q17 · Light & sound
Why do we see lightning before we hear the thunder?
  1. Our eyes are faster than our ears
  2. Sound needs light to travel
  3. Thunder happens later
  4. Light travels much faster than sound
Why: Light travels at about 300 000 km/s; sound in air only about 340 m/s.
Q18 · Light & sound
Sound cannot travel through which of these?
  1. A vacuum
  2. Steel
  3. Air
  4. Water
Why: Sound is a vibration passed between particles. A vacuum has no particles, so sound cannot travel through it.
Q19 · Working scientifically
A student tests how the height of a ramp affects how far a car rolls. What is the independent variable?
  1. How far the car rolls
  2. The type of floor
  3. The mass of the car
  4. The height of the ramp
Why: The independent variable is the one you change on purpose. The distance rolled is the dependent variable you measure.
Q20 · Working scientifically
Why should you repeat an experiment several times?
  1. To make it take longer
  2. To change the variables
  3. To check results are repeatable and spot anomalies
  4. To use more equipment
Why: Repeating lets you calculate a mean, spot odd results and check your results are reliable.

Year 7 Maths: Algebra basics

WORDS TO KNOW
• Term: $3a$, $5$, $-2b$
• Expression: terms added or subtracted, e.g. $3a + 5b - 2$ (no equals sign)
• Equation: has an equals sign and can be solved, e.g. $2x + 5 = 17$

SIMPLIFYING (collect like terms)
Only combine terms with the same letter.
$3a + 5b - a + 2b = 2a + 7b$

SUBSTITUTION
Swap the letter for the number, then follow BIDMAS.
If $x = 4$: $3x^2 = 3 \times 16 = 48$ (square first, then multiply)

SOLVING ONE- AND TWO-STEP EQUATIONS
Do the same thing to both sides, undoing in reverse order.
$2x + 5 = 17$
subtract 5: $2x = 12$
divide by 2: $x = 6$
Check: $2 \times 6 + 5 = 17$ ✓

SEQUENCES
3, 7, 11, 15, … goes up by 4 each time.
$n$th term: $4n - 1$ (the times-table of the difference, then adjust)

ANGLE FACTS
• Angles on a straight line add up to 180°
• Angles around a point add up to 360°
• Angles in a triangle add up to 180°
• Vertically opposite angles are equal

Example: a triangle has angles 48° and 75°. Third angle = $180 - 48 - 75 = 57$°

TYPES OF ANGLE
acute: less than 90° right: 90° obtuse: between 90° and 180° reflex: more than 180°

The distance all the way around. Add every side.

Year 7 Maths: Fractions, decimals and percentages

KEY IDEA
Fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways of writing the same amount.
$\dfrac{1}{2} = 0.5 = 50\%$ $\dfrac{1}{4} = 0.25 = 25\%$ $\dfrac{3}{4} = 0.75 = 75\%$ $\dfrac{1}{10} = 0.1 = 10\%$ $\dfrac{1}{5} = 0.2 = 20\%$

CONVERTING
• Fraction → decimal: divide the top by the bottom. $\dfrac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8 = 0.375$
• Decimal → percentage: multiply by 100. $0.375 = 37.5\%$
• Percentage → fraction: put it over 100 and simplify. $35\% = \dfrac{35}{100} = \dfrac{7}{20}$

ADDING FRACTIONS
Make the denominators the same first.
$\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{8}{12} + \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{11}{12}$

FRACTION OF AN AMOUNT
Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.
$\dfrac{3}{5}$ of 40: $40 \div 5 = 8$, then $8 \times 3 = 24$

PERCENTAGE OF AN AMOUNT (no calculator)
Build it from 10% and 5%.
15% of 60: 10% = 6, 5% = 3, so 15% = 9

COMMON MISTAKE
Adding the tops and the bottoms: $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3}$ is NOT $\dfrac{2}{5}$. It is $\dfrac{3}{6} + \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{5}{6}$.

Year 7 Maths: Negative numbers

ON THE NUMBER LINE
Numbers to the left are smaller: $-7 < -2 < 0 < 3$.
Think of temperatures: $-7$°C is colder than $-2$°C.

ADDING AND SUBTRACTING
• Adding a positive: move right. $-4 + 9 = 5$
• Subtracting a positive: move left. $3 - 8 = -5$
• Two signs next to each other:
$+\,-$ or $-\,+$ → minus. $6 + (-2) = 6 - 2 = 4$
$-\,-$ → plus. $5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8$

MULTIPLYING AND DIVIDING
Same signs → positive. Different signs → negative.
$-3 \times -6 = 18$ $-20 \div 4 = -5$ $7 \times -2 = -14$

CHECK YOURSELF
$-2 - 5 = ?$ (answer: $-7$)
$-12 \div -3 = ?$ (answer: 4)

Year 7 Science: Forces and energy

FORCES
A force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons (N).
Contact forces: friction, air resistance, tension, normal (support) force
Non-contact forces: gravity (weight), magnetism, static electricity

RESULTANT FORCE
• Same direction: add. Opposite directions: subtract.
10 N right and 4 N left → resultant 6 N to the right.
• Balanced forces (resultant 0): the object stays still or keeps moving at the same speed.

MASS AND WEIGHT
Mass is the amount of matter (kg) and does not change.
Weight is the force of gravity (N): weight = mass × gravitational field strength
On Earth $g \approx 10$ N/kg, so a 50 kg pupil weighs 500 N. On the Moon they weigh about one sixth as much.

ENERGY STORES
kinetic (moving), gravitational (high up), elastic (stretched), chemical (food, fuel, batteries), thermal (hot), nuclear
Energy is never used up; it is transferred between stores.

RENEWABLE vs NON-RENEWABLE
Renewable: wind, solar, tidal, hydroelectric, geothermal
Non-renewable: coal, oil, natural gas, nuclear

Year 7 Science: Particles and states of matter

THE PARTICLE MODEL
Everything is made of tiny particles. How they are arranged decides the state.

SOLID: particles touching, in a regular pattern, vibrate on the spot. Fixed shape, cannot be squashed.
LIQUID: particles touching but arranged randomly, can slide past each other. Flows, takes the shape of its container, cannot be squashed.
GAS: particles far apart, moving quickly in all directions. Fills its container, can be squashed.

CHANGES OF STATE
solid → liquid: melting
liquid → gas: boiling / evaporating
gas → liquid: condensing
liquid → solid: freezing
solid → gas: subliming

DIFFUSION
Particles spread out from where there are lots to where there are few. It is faster in gases and when hotter.

MIXTURES
• Filtration: separates an insoluble solid from a liquid (sand and water)
• Evaporation: gets a dissolved solid back (salt from salty water)
• Chromatography: separates coloured dyes in ink

Year 8 Maths: Expanding, factorising and equations

EXPANDING BRACKETS
Multiply everything inside by the term outside.
$5(x + 3) = 5x + 15$ $-2(x - 4) = -2x + 8$ (negative × negative = positive)

FACTORISING (the reverse)
Take out the highest common factor.
$12x + 18 = 6(2x + 3)$ $x^2 + 7x = x(x + 7)$

EQUATIONS WITH x ON BOTH SIDES
Get the x terms on one side first.
$7x - 4 = 3x + 20$
subtract $3x$: $4x - 4 = 20$
add 4: $4x = 24$
divide by 4: $x = 6$

EQUATIONS WITH BRACKETS
Expand first. $4(x - 2) = 2x + 6$ → $4x - 8 = 2x + 6$ → $2x = 14$ → $x = 7$

nth TERM
Difference first, then adjust. 11, 8, 5, 2: difference $-3$, so $14 - 3n$.
Check with $n = 1$: $14 - 3 = 11$ ✓

Year 8 Science: Atoms, elements, compounds and reactions

ATOMS AND ELEMENTS
Everything is made of atoms. An element contains only one type of atom (iron, oxygen, carbon).
The periodic table lists all the elements. Metals: left and middle. Non-metals: right.
Elements in the same GROUP (column) react in similar ways.

COMPOUNDS
Two or more elements chemically joined. Water $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: 2 hydrogen + 1 oxygen.
Carbon dioxide $\text{CO}_2$: 1 carbon + 2 oxygen.

MIXTURE vs COMPOUND
Mixture: not joined, easy to separate (air, salt water).
Compound: chemically joined, new properties (water, salt).

CHEMICAL REACTIONS
reactants → products
magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide
Mass is conserved: atoms are rearranged, never created or destroyed.

SIGNS OF A REACTION
gas given off, colour change, temperature change, new solid (precipitate) forms

Year 9 Maths: Graphs, Pythagoras and percentages

STRAIGHT-LINE GRAPHS: $y = mx + c$
$m$ = gradient (steepness), $c$ = where it crosses the $y$-axis.
Gradient from two points = change in $y$ ÷ change in $x$
(1, 3) to (4, 12): $9 \div 3 = 3$

PYTHAGORAS (right-angled triangles only)
$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, where $c$ is the hypotenuse (longest side, opposite the right angle)
Finding $c$: square, ADD, square root. 6 and 8 → $\sqrt{100} = 10$
Finding a shorter side: square, SUBTRACT, square root. 13 and 12 → $\sqrt{25} = 5$

VOLUME OF A PRISM / CYLINDER
Volume = area of cross-section × length. Cylinder: $\pi r^2 h$

REVERSE PERCENTAGES
After a 20% increase the price is £60. £60 = 120%, so 100% = $60 \div 1.2 = $ £50

COMPOUND INTEREST
original × (multiplier)$^{\text{years}}$: $1000 \times 1.1^2 = 1210$

Year 9 Science: Genetics, energy and forces

DNA AND GENES
DNA is in the chromosomes inside the nucleus. A gene is a section of DNA that codes for a characteristic.
Body cells: 46 chromosomes (23 pairs). Sex cells: 23.

VARIATION AND EVOLUTION
Inherited (eye colour) vs environmental (scars, language).
Natural selection: the best-adapted individuals survive, reproduce and pass on their genes.

ENERGY
Energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h)
Efficiency = useful energy out ÷ total energy in (500 J in, 400 J useful → 80%)

ELECTRICITY
$P = I \times V$ (power = current × potential difference)

FORCES AND MOTION
$F = m \times a$ (force = mass × acceleration)
Density = mass ÷ volume

WAVES
Sound: longitudinal. Light and all EM waves: transverse.
EM spectrum: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma

Year 9 Science: Atomic structure and equations

INSIDE THE ATOM
Nucleus: protons (+1) and neutrons (0). Shells: electrons (−1).
Atomic number = number of protons. Neutral atom: protons = electrons.

THE PERIODIC TABLE
Group 1 (alkali metals): very reactive, react with water to make hydrogen. More reactive going DOWN.
Group 7 (halogens): reactive non-metals. Less reactive going down.
Group 0 (noble gases): unreactive, full outer shells.

BALANCING EQUATIONS
Same number of each atom on both sides; change only the big numbers in front.
$2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (4 H and 2 O each side)

REACTIVITY SERIES (most → least)
potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium, (carbon), zinc, iron, copper, silver, gold

THE ATMOSPHERE
78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, about 1% argon, about 0.04% carbon dioxide.
More $\text{CO}_2$ traps more heat → global warming.
