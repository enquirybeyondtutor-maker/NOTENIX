GCSE Biology diagnostic

Part 1: Paper 1 topics · 8 questions · 10 min

gcse-biology-diagnostic-p1-01 · Cells and organisation · Cell structure and transport · difficulty 1
Which of these structures is found in a plant cell but not in an animal cell?
  1. Nucleus
  2. Mitochondria
  3. Cell wall
  4. Ribosomes
Answer C. Plant cells have a cell wall made of cellulose that supports the cell. Animal cells have a nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes too, but no cell wall.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p1-02 · Cells and organisation · Cell structure and transport · difficulty 2
Water moves from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane. What is this process called?
  1. Osmosis
  2. Active transport
  3. Diffusion
  4. Transpiration
Answer A. Osmosis is the movement of water across a partially permeable membrane from a dilute solution to a more concentrated one. Diffusion is the wider term for any particles spreading out, and active transport needs energy.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p1-03 · Cells and organisation · Cell division · difficulty 2
A human body cell has 46 chromosomes. It divides by mitosis. How many chromosomes does each new cell have?
  1. 23
  2. 92
  3. 12
  4. 46
Answer D. Mitosis makes two genetically identical cells, so each has the full set of 46 chromosomes. Only meiosis, which makes gametes, halves the number to 23.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p1-04 · Cells and organisation · Organisation and digestion · difficulty 2
Which enzyme breaks down starch into sugars?
  1. Lipase
  2. Amylase
  3. Protease
  4. Bile
Answer B. Amylase breaks starch down into sugars. Lipase works on fats and protease works on proteins. Bile is not an enzyme: it emulsifies fats.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p1-05 · Cells and organisation · Heart, blood and circulation · difficulty 2
Which blood vessel carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart?
  1. Pulmonary vein
  2. Pulmonary artery
  3. Aorta
  4. Vena cava
Answer A. The pulmonary vein brings oxygenated blood from the lungs back to the left atrium. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood the other way, from the heart to the lungs.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p1-06 · Infection and bioenergetics · Infection and response · difficulty 2
A patient has influenza (flu). Why will antibiotics not cure it?
  1. Flu is caused by a fungus
  2. The patient is already immune to antibiotics
  3. Flu is caused by a virus, and antibiotics only kill bacteria
  4. Antibiotics only work on the skin
Answer C. Antibiotics kill bacteria. Flu is caused by a virus, which reproduces inside the body's own cells, so antibiotics have no effect on it.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p1-07 · Infection and bioenergetics · Photosynthesis · difficulty 1
What are the two products of photosynthesis?
  1. Carbon dioxide and water
  2. Glucose and oxygen
  3. Glucose and carbon dioxide
  4. Oxygen and water
Answer B. Photosynthesis uses light energy to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen. Carbon dioxide and water are the reactants, not the products.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p1-08 · Infection and bioenergetics · Respiration · difficulty 3
During hard exercise, muscle cells may respire anaerobically. What does anaerobic respiration in muscle cells produce?
  1. Ethanol and carbon dioxide
  2. Carbon dioxide and water
  3. Glucose and oxygen
  4. Lactic acid
Answer D. Without enough oxygen, glucose in muscle cells is only partly broken down, producing lactic acid. Ethanol and carbon dioxide are made by yeast and plant cells, not by muscles.

Part 2: Paper 2 topics · 8 questions · 10 min

gcse-biology-diagnostic-p2-01 · Control and inheritance · Homeostasis and hormones · difficulty 2
Which hormone lowers the concentration of glucose in the blood?
  1. Glucagon
  2. Adrenaline
  3. Insulin
  4. Thyroxine
Answer C. Insulin, released by the pancreas, causes cells to take in glucose and the liver to store it as glycogen, so blood glucose falls. Glucagon does the opposite.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p2-02 · Control and inheritance · Nervous system · difficulty 2
Which is the correct order for a reflex arc?
  1. Receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone, effector
  2. Effector, motor neurone, relay neurone, sensory neurone, receptor
  3. Receptor, motor neurone, relay neurone, sensory neurone, effector
  4. Receptor, relay neurone, sensory neurone, effector, motor neurone
Answer A. A receptor detects the stimulus, a sensory neurone carries the impulse to the spinal cord, a relay neurone passes it on, and a motor neurone carries it to the effector, a muscle or gland.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p2-03 · Control and inheritance · Inheritance and genetics · difficulty 3
Cystic fibrosis is caused by a recessive allele. Both parents are carriers (heterozygous). What is the probability that their next child has cystic fibrosis?
  1. 50%
  2. 75%
  3. 0%
  4. 25%
Answer D. Each parent passes on the recessive allele with probability one half. The child needs two recessive alleles, so the probability is one half times one half, which is one quarter or 25%.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p2-04 · Control and inheritance · Inheritance and genetics · difficulty 1
Which sex chromosomes are found in the body cells of a human male?
  1. XX
  2. XY
  3. YY
  4. X only
Answer B. Human males have one X and one Y chromosome in each body cell. Females have two X chromosomes.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p2-05 · Control and inheritance · Variation and evolution · difficulty 2
A population of bacteria becomes resistant to an antibiotic. What is the first step in this process?
  1. A random mutation gives some bacteria resistance
  2. The bacteria decide to change when they meet the antibiotic
  3. The antibiotic makes every bacterium mutate in the same way
  4. Patients become immune to the antibiotic
Answer A. Resistance starts with a random mutation in a few bacteria. When the antibiotic is used, those bacteria survive and reproduce while the others die, so the resistant strain spreads.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p2-06 · Ecology · Ecology · difficulty 2
In the food chain grass, rabbit, fox, what is the rabbit?
  1. Producer
  2. Secondary consumer
  3. Primary consumer
  4. Decomposer
Answer C. The grass is the producer. The rabbit eats the producer, so it is a primary consumer. The fox, which eats the rabbit, is a secondary consumer.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p2-07 · Ecology · Ecology · difficulty 3
A student places $10$ quadrats, each of area $0.25\ \text{m}^2$, at random in a field and counts a total of $40$ daisies. The field has an area of $200\ \text{m}^2$. What is the best estimate of the number of daisies in the field?
  1. 800
  2. 3200
  3. 8000
  4. 320
Answer B. The area sampled is $10 \times 0.25 = 2.5\ \text{m}^2$, so there are $40 \div 2.5 = 16$ daisies per square metre. For the whole field: $16 \times 200 = 3200$.
gcse-biology-diagnostic-p2-08 · Control and inheritance · Homeostasis and hormones · difficulty 2
What happens in the skin when body temperature rises too high?
  1. Shivering starts
  2. Blood vessels near the skin surface narrow
  3. Hairs on the skin stand up
  4. Blood vessels near the skin surface widen
Answer D. When the body is too hot, blood vessels near the skin surface widen (vasodilation) so more blood flows there and more energy is lost. Sweating also increases. The other three responses happen when the body is too cold.

Which part of a cell controls which substances enter and leave it?

What is diffusion?
  1. The movement of water only across a membrane
  2. The movement of particles against a concentration gradient, using energy
  3. The movement of particles from a lower to a higher concentration
  4. The net movement of particles from a higher to a lower concentration
Answer D. Diffusion is the spreading out of particles, giving a net movement from where they are more concentrated to where they are less concentrated. It needs no energy from the cell.

Which part of a cell is the site of protein synthesis?
  1. Ribosome
  2. Mitochondrion

  4. Chloroplast
Answer A. Proteins are made on ribosomes. Mitochondria are where aerobic respiration happens, and chloroplasts are where photosynthesis happens.

What is the process called by which a cell becomes specialised for a particular job?
  1. Mitosis
  2. Meiosis
  3. Differentiation
  4. Diffusion
Answer C. Differentiation is when a cell gains the structures it needs to carry out a particular function. Most animal cells differentiate early in development, but many plant cells keep the ability throughout life.
gcse-biology-mock-1-p1-05 · Cells and organisation · Organisation and digestion · difficulty 1
Which organ produces bile?
  1. Gall bladder

Why do veins contain valves?

gcse-biology-mock-1-p1-12 · Infection and bioenergetics · Respiration · difficulty 2
Which statement correctly compares aerobic and anaerobic respiration in human muscle?

gcse-biology-mock-1-p2-01 · Control and inheritance · Homeostasis and hormones · difficulty 1
What is homeostasis?
  1. The response of an organism to a stimulus
  2. The production of hormones by glands
  3. Keeping the body's internal conditions within set limits
  4. The transport of substances in the blood
Answer C. Homeostasis is the regulation of internal conditions, such as body temperature, blood glucose and water levels, to keep them at the best levels for cells and enzymes to work.

How are hormones carried around the body?
  1. In the blood plasma
  2. Along neurones as electrical impulses
  3. Through the digestive system
  4. In the air in the lungs
Answer A. Hormones are chemicals released by glands directly into the bloodstream. They travel in the blood plasma to their target organs, so their effects are slower than nerve impulses.
gcse-biology-mock-1-p2-03 · Control and inheritance · Homeostasis and hormones · difficulty 2
How does a contraceptive pill containing oestrogen and progesterone prevent pregnancy?
  1. It kills sperm
  2. It forms a barrier at the cervix
  3. It makes the lining of the uterus thicker
  4. It stops FSH production, so no eggs mature
Answer D. The hormones in the pill inhibit the production of FSH, so eggs do not mature and are not released. Spermicides kill sperm, and barrier methods stop sperm reaching the egg.
gcse-biology-mock-1-p2-04 · Control and inheritance · Nervous system · difficulty 1
What is a change in the environment that an organism can detect called?
  1. A response

gcse-biology-mock-1-p2-05 · Control and inheritance · Nervous system · difficulty 2
In the nervous system, what are effectors?
  1. Muscles or glands that bring about a response
  2. Cells that detect stimuli

Answer A. Effectors are muscles, which contract, and glands, which release hormones. They respond to impulses carried by motor neurones.
gcse-biology-mock-1-p2-06 · Control and inheritance · Inheritance and genetics · difficulty 1
Which molecule carries the genetic information in cells?
  1. Glucose

gcse-biology-mock-1-p2-07 · Control and inheritance · Inheritance and genetics · difficulty 2
What is a dominant allele?

  3. An allele that is found only in males

gcse-biology-mock-1-p2-08 · Control and inheritance · Inheritance and genetics · difficulty 3

Why are root hair cells well adapted to absorb water from the soil?

Answer A. The long, thin extension of a root hair cell gives a large surface area for water to move in by osmosis and for mineral ions to be absorbed by active transport. They are underground, so they have no chloroplasts.
gcse-biology-mock-2-p1-02 · Cells and organisation · Cell structure and transport · difficulty 3
A cell is $25\ \mu\text{m}$ long. A photograph shows it magnified 400 times. How long is the cell in the photograph?
  1. 0.0625 mm
  2. 1 mm
  3. 10 mm
  4. 100 mm
Answer C. Image size $=$ real size $\times$ magnification $= 25 \times 400 = 10\,000\ \mu\text{m}$. There are $1000\ \mu\text{m}$ in 1 mm, so the image is 10 mm long.
gcse-biology-mock-2-p1-03 · Cells and organisation · Cell structure and transport · difficulty 3
A cylinder of potato is placed in a concentrated sugar solution. After an hour its mass has decreased. Why?
  1. Sugar moved into the potato by diffusion
  2. Water moved out of the potato cells by osmosis
  3. Water moved into the potato cells by active transport
  4. The potato cells used the sugar in respiration
Answer B. The solution outside is more concentrated than the cell contents, so water moves out of the cells by osmosis, through the partially permeable membranes. The loss of water reduces the mass.

Answer C. The active site has a specific shape. Only a substrate whose shape is complementary to it can fit and react, so each enzyme is specific to one reaction. Enzymes are not used up.

gcse-biology-mock-2-p1-10 · Infection and bioenergetics · Infection and response · difficulty 2

gcse-biology-mock-2-p2-06 · Control and inheritance · Inheritance and genetics · difficulty 3

  4. Four cells, all genetically identical
